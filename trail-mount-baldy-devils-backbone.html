Mount Baldy via Devil's Backbone

A demanding climb to 10,064-foot Mount San Antonio, using the broad Notch approach and an exposed ridge above the Los Angeles basin.

CLIMATE

How the year behaves

This is high-elevation mountain terrain with cold winters, persistent snow and ice, strong ridge wind, and large temperature swings. Even in summer, the summit can be much colder than Manker Flat.

Best season: Snow-free conditions for experienced hikers

WATER SOURCE

Carry all water

Seasonal falls and drainages are not a dependable or potable supply. Carry enough from the trailhead for the full climb and descent; do not assume services at the Notch will be open.

This is one of Southern California's defining summit days and one of its least forgiving. The route combines a long fire-road climb, high-elevation exposure, loose trail, and almost 4,000 feet of gain before a steep descent through Baldy Bowl.

ROUTE WALKTHROUGH

What the hike is like

  1. From Manker Flat, climb the paved and dirt service road past San Antonio Falls toward Baldy Notch.
  2. Continue above the Notch to the Devil's Backbone ridge, then follow the crest toward Mount Harwood and the Mount Baldy summit.
  3. A common loop descends the Baldy Bowl/Ski Hut Trail. It is steep, loose, and easy to underestimate late in the day.

BEFORE YOU DRIVE

Planning the day

  • Treat the listed mileage as a full mountain day and preserve time, water, and leg strength for the descent.
  • Weather at 10,000 feet can be dramatically colder and windier than the trailhead.
  • Verify current Angeles National Forest orders, road access, and ski-area operations before leaving.

TURN-AROUND JUDGMENT

Safety that matters here

  • Winter and spring snow turn this into mountaineering terrain. Microspikes alone are not a substitute for ice-axe, crampon, and self-arrest skills when slopes are firm.
  • The Devil's Backbone is exposed. Avoid the ridge in high wind, lightning, ice, or when your group is uncomfortable with drop-offs.
  • Altitude illness can begin below the summit. Descend if headache, nausea, confusion, or poor coordination develops.
