A long canyon day with repeated river crossings, rugged route-finding, and an abandoned 1930s bridge deep in the East Fork.

CLIMATE

How the year behaves

The canyon is coolest from late fall through spring. Summer combines strong reflected heat, exposed gravel, and limited shade; winter storms can transform a moderate river into an impassable barrier.

Best season: Cool, dry days from fall through spring

Bridge to Nowhere feels more like a small expedition than a conventional footpath. The route repeatedly crosses the East Fork of the San Gabriel River, threads through flood-scoured canyon, and ends at a concrete arch left behind by an unfinished mountain road.

ROUTE WALKTHROUGH

What the hike is like

  1. Begin at the East Fork Trailhead and follow the river upstream toward Sheep Mountain Wilderness.
  2. Expect multiple unbridged crossings. The exact line shifts after storms, so painted arrows and old road traces are only partial guidance.
  3. The bridge stands on a private inholding. Respect signs, stay on the public route, and return the way you came.

BEFORE YOU DRIVE

Planning the day

  • Arrive early: the East Fork corridor is heavily visited and roadside parking restrictions are enforced.
  • Wear footwear that can handle repeated water crossings and pack dry socks in a waterproof bag.
  • Check the Angeles National Forest alerts page before driving. Fire recovery, road work, and forest orders can affect access.

TURN-AROUND JUDGMENT

Safety that matters here

  • Do not enter the canyon when storms or flash flooding are possible. River volume can make crossings unsafe.
  • Summer heat is severe and shade is inconsistent. Carry more water than the mileage alone suggests.
  • Navigation becomes harder after flood damage; turn around if you cannot identify a safe crossing or return line.
