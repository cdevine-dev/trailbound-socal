Trail guide

8 Southern California hikes that belong on your short list

A landscape-spanning starting point for hikers who want coast, desert, river canyon, and high-mountain days.

BY TRAILBOUND SOCAL8 min read

Start with the day you actually want

Southern California can feel like several worlds stitched together. Choose a breezy coastal loop, a river canyon, an exposed desert summit, or a high-elevation climb before you choose the most famous trail name.

Coast: Sandstone Peak and Crystal Cove

Sandstone Peak brings Pacific-facing ridges and sculpted rock to the Santa Monica Mountains. Crystal Cove connects coastal sage backcountry with a beach finish. Both are exposed, so cool mornings and current closure checks matter.

San Gabriels: Bridge to Nowhere and Mount Baldy

Bridge to Nowhere is a long river-canyon day with shifting crossings. Mount Baldy is a serious 10,000-foot summit where wind, altitude, and winter ice demand conservative decisions. Neither is a casual mileage-only outing.

Desert and high country

Ryan Mountain offers a compact thousand-foot climb in Joshua Tree. Borrego Palm Canyon introduces fan-palm habitat and desert geology, although the palm grove is currently closed beyond the recovery overlook. San Jacinto via the tram is still an 11.6-mile high-altitude day.

Make the short list personal

Match the route to the least-experienced person in the group. Account for heat, altitude, daylight, water, parking, and driving time. A modest trail at the right hour is better than a bucket-list objective forced into the wrong day.

BEFORE YOU GO

Conditions and access change. Check official agency alerts, weather, closures, permits, and fire restrictions before every hike.
