Trail culture

Small choices that protect Southern California trails

Practical trail etiquette for popular routes and fragile landscapes.

BY TRAILBOUND SOCAL6 min read

Stay on durable routes

Cutting switchbacks widens erosion and damages slow-growing vegetation. Walk single-file where trails narrow and step carefully when yielding.

Leave the place intact

Pack out food scraps, tissues, pet waste, and every item you carry in. Leave plants, rocks, cultural objects, and wildlife undisturbed.

Respect closures

A gate or closure sign may protect a washed-out trail, fire recovery, wildlife habitat, or rescue crews. Do not route around it because an older app track says the path continues.

Share the trail

Keep sound low, follow posted pet rules, yield thoughtfully, and avoid blocking the route for photos. Popular overlooks work better when everyone keeps moving.

Treat the official source as current

Trip reports are useful context, but land managers control hours, permits, closures, and fire rules. Check their page again on the morning of the hike.

BEFORE YOU GO

Conditions and access change. Check official agency alerts, weather, closures, permits, and fire restrictions before every hike.
